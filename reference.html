1. The BTC market ladder on Kalshi

Kalshi doesn't run one Bitcoin market — it runs the same underlying question at six different speeds. All of them settle against the same reference price family (CF Benchmarks' Bitcoin Real-Time Index, averaged over the final 60 seconds of the window), so the mechanics are consistent — only the window length changes.

FrequencyTickerWhat it asks
15-minuteKXBTC15MUp or down vs. the strike at window open
HourlyKXBTCDAbove or below a strike price
Daily / Weekly / Monthly(rotating)Same above/below structure, longer windows
YearlyKXBTCY / KXBTCMAXY / KXBTCMINYYear-end price, and yearly max/min thresholds

Ticker names occasionally rotate — always confirm the live ticker on Kalshi's own market page before trusting an automated fetch.

2. How settlement actually works

  • Every window settles against CF Benchmarks' Real-Time Index, not a single exchange's last-traded price.
  • The reference value is a 60-second average right before the window opens, and another 60-second average right before it closes — not an instantaneous snapshot.
  • Real-time, licensed access to that exact index feed is not freely available — it requires a commercial agreement with CF Benchmarks. Our tool approximates spot price using free feeds (CryptoCompare, CoinGecko), which will diverge slightly from Kalshi's own settlement price at any given instant.

3. About Kalshi itself

  • Founded 2018, launched publicly in July 2021. Based in Manhattan, NY.
  • Regulated by the CFTC as an event-contract exchange — legally distinct from a sportsbook or a brokerage.
  • Sports now make up 90%+ of Kalshi's total activity and revenue — crypto, weather, economics, and politics are meaningfully smaller categories by volume.
  • Public read-only market data (prices, volumes) is available with no login via external-api.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2 — this is what our own tools use.
  • Trading fees exist and are non-trivial — we have not built real fee modeling into our paper-trading numbers yet. Every profit figure we've calculated so far is a slight overestimate versus what real trading would net.

5. How to read any of these stats

  • 5 samples — the floor before a stat shows anything at all. Still meaningless on its own.
  • ~30 samples — a real number starts to exist, but the honest range around it is still wide (±15-20 points).
  • ~100 samples — a stable-looking edge here is worth taking seriously.
  • Win rate ≠ profit. A strategy that wins 80% of the time on an 85¢ favorite can still lose money; one correct wrong-way bet erases several right ones.
  • Every stat here has a confidence range for a reason — a headline percentage without one is a number chosen to look convincing, not to be true.
